Product roadmap · 12 months · Spend Lens

A roadmap is a set of bets, not a list of dates.

A 12-month roadmap for Spend Lens, my privacy-first spending app, treated as a startup with two engineers, a designer and me. Strategy first, then 16 initiatives scored with the framework you choose, placed into Now, Next and Later by score, capacity and dependencies. Change any score and watch the plan move.

All scores are my own estimates for a product with no users yet. Reach is the share of active households affected in a quarter, impact is on a 0.25 to 3 scale, confidence is how sure I am, effort is person-weeks.

What the scores say, and where I overrule them

Under all three frameworks, with my default estimates, the Couples bet lands in Later: shared budgets are big and uncertain, so they score low. Cheap, safe habit work fills Now. That is the classic trap of scoring: it favours small certain wins over the strategic bet. My call would be to keep Now as scored, then pin Shared budget from two exports to Next and run interviews first (Listening Tour) to raise its confidence or kill it. Try the pin in the editor and see what it pushes out.

Quarter plan

Initiatives fill each quarter in score order until capacity runs out, and never before what they depend on. Now is Q1, Next is Q2, Later is Q3 and Q4.

How I would run it

  • Now is a commitment, Next is a plan, Later is a direction. Only Now has scope and owners. Later items are problems, not features.
  • Review every six weeks against the outcome metrics, not against delivery dates.
  • Scores start debates, they do not end them. When the framework changes the order a lot, the inputs are uncertain. That is where research goes first.
  • Each initiative names the metric it should move. If it does not move it after launch, it leaves the roadmap.
